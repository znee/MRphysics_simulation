What You're Seeing

Without gradients, all spins have the same phase (pointing same direction). Toggle gradients to see how they create position-dependent phase patterns.

Key Difference

Gx (Frequency): ON during readout → phase accumulates continuously → oscillating signal
Gy (Phase): ON before readout, then OFF → fixed phase imprint → constant magnitude

What You're Seeing

Acquire: every voxel sends its own signal (frequency set by x, starting phase by ky·y). The coil records only their sum: each sum is one k-space sample; one readout fills one ky line.
Decompose: each k-space value is the weight of one stripe pattern (spatial frequency). The inverse Fourier transform adds the patterns back, center first: low k gives the blurry shape and contrast, high k gives the edges.

What You're Seeing

With Gz on, the Larmor frequency changes linearly with position: f(z) = γ̄·Gz·z. An RF pulse only tips spins whose frequency falls inside its bandwidth (resonance), so it excites one slab. Thinner slice: stronger Gz or narrower BW (longer pulse). Move the slice: shift the RF center frequency.

RF Pulse ↔ Slice Profile

The slice profile is (approximately) the Fourier transform of the RF envelope: a sinc pulse gives a rectangular slab. Truncating the sinc (fewer lobes) rounds the edges.

\(FOV = \dfrac{BW}{\bar{\gamma}\, G_x}, \quad \Delta x = \dfrac{FOV}{N}\)

What You're Seeing

During readout every position "sings its own pitch": f(x) = γ̄·Gx·x. The coil hears only the chord (the sum). A Fourier transform splits the chord back into its notes — the strength of each frequency is the amount of tissue at that position.

Try This

• Increase N (longer readout) → finer Δx.
• Lower BW or raise Gx → smaller FOV.
• "Object wider than FOV": parts outside the FOV wrap to the other side (aliasing).
